For a lot of working families in Florida, healthcare feels like something you only deal with when things get bad enough.

You work. You pay bills. You try to stay ahead. But somewhere in the middle of rent, groceries, gas, childcare, and everything else, healthcare keeps getting pushed further down the list.

Not because people do not care about their health. Most do.

The problem is that many working adults earn too much to qualify for public assistance programs, while still not having realistic access to affordable care options. That space in the middle is what many people call the working gap, and it affects more families than most people realize.

That is the problem BEMA was created to help address.

## The "Working Gap" Most People Never Talk About

There are thousands of people across Florida who technically have jobs but still struggle to access consistent healthcare services.

Some are self-employed. Some work hourly jobs. Some are small business owners. Some work full time but still cannot comfortably manage the cost of routine healthcare expenses.

A lot of these families end up doing the same thing. They wait.

They postpone checkups. Ignore symptoms. Stretch prescriptions. Avoid eye exams. Put off counseling. Hope things improve on their own.

Not because they are irresponsible, but because every financial decision feels connected to survival.

And when healthcare starts feeling unpredictable or confusing, it becomes easier to avoid it entirely.

## Why Many Working Families Delay Care

For many people, the issue is not just cost. It is also the experience.

Long wait times. Short visits. Confusing pricing. Feeling rushed. Feeling like nobody explains anything clearly.

When someone already feels financially stretched, one frustrating experience can be enough to make them stop trying altogether.

That is especially true for parents trying to balance work schedules, childcare, transportation, and everyday life.

Healthcare becomes reactive instead of proactive. People wait until something feels urgent because routine care feels too difficult to navigate consistently.

Over time, that creates bigger problems for families and communities alike.

## The Problem Is Bigger Than One Doctor Visit

Healthcare access is connected to almost everything else in life.

If someone cannot access routine primary care, they may miss work more often. If they avoid eye care, it can affect driving, school, or job performance. If mental health support feels unreachable, stress can slowly affect relationships, sleep, focus, and daily life.

These are not isolated problems.

For many working Floridians, the challenge is not finding one appointment. The challenge is finding a healthcare experience that feels practical, understandable, and sustainable long term.

That is why coordinated access matters.

## How BEMA Coordinates Access Differently

BEMA is a Florida nonprofit organization focused on helping working individuals and families connect with healthcare services through a network of participating partner clinics.

BEMA does not operate clinics or provide medical care directly.

Instead, the organization helps coordinate access to services like direct primary care, eye care, and licensed mental health counseling through trusted healthcare partners.

The goal is simple. Reduce friction. Make access easier to understand. Help working families find practical paths to care without making the process feel overwhelming.

Some services may be available through telehealth across Florida, while in-person partner services are currently centered in Central Florida.

For many families, just having a clearer and more approachable starting point makes a real difference.

Healthcare should not feel out of reach simply because someone is stuck in the middle.
